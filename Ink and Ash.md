# **Master Game Design Document: The Ink and the Ash**

### **1\. Game Overview, Vision, & Philosophical Rationale**

#### **1.1 Executive Summary & Core Identity**

*The Ink and the Ash* is a cozy-melancholic JRPG and open-world pilgrimage designed to redefine core conflict resolution loops within systemic role-playing games. Set within a continent suffering under the rigid administrative occupation of the Iron Ledger Empire, the project establishes a narrative ethos centered on restorative stewardship rather than military conquest. Players navigate a world where true resistance is achieved not through armies, siege craft or violence, but through community care, shared meals, mutual support and restorative mediation.

The game synthesizes classic turn-based tactical depth with immersive travel logistics. By replacing conventional conquest mechanics with environmental and social healing loops, *The Ink and the Ash* constructs a world state where repairing community ties renders imperial authority obsolete. The visual presentation reinforces this narrative thesis by contrasting warm, welcoming domestic sanctuaries against cold, mechanical imperial structures.

| Aesthetic Realm | Visual Elements & Asset Usage | Emotional & Narrative Function |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Cozy Village Aesthetic** | Built with Synty Fantasy Village and Synty Biomes asset packs featuring warm timber houses, stone hearths, pastoral greenery and rustic safety. | Establishes an immediate sense of emotional sanctuary, domestic safety and community warmth. |
| **Imperial Architecture** | Characterized by austere stamped-iron architecture, rigid geometric lines, monochrome metals and heavy administrative posts. | Evokes cold bureaucratic control, oppressive stasis, environmental disruption and emotional isolation. |

#### **1.2 Theological & Philosophical Framework: The Way of the Unbound Thread**

The protagonist operates as a licensed Vicar-Mediator whose practice stems from *The Way of the Unbound Thread*. This living faith rejects dogmatic theology and rigid doctrine, focusing instead on active listening, reducing suffering, exercising restorative stewardship and cultivating radical interdependence across all living communities.

The central ideological conflict pits the philosophical reality of *"All Things Pass"* (*Anicca*) against Imperial Stasis:

* **Imperial Stasis:** The occupying Iron Ledger Empire makes the fundamental error of attempting to freeze time and combat natural decay. It enforces control through rigid administrative ledgers, oppressive taxation, stamped-iron architecture and bureaucratic mandates.  
* **The Way of the Unbound Thread (*Anicca*):** The Vicar-Mediator acknowledges that all structures, empires and hardships are transient. Rather than seeking cold non-attachment or resisting change, the protagonist flows with natural transitions, easing regional grief and decay through mindful stewardship and active care.

The empire's primary systemic weapon is isolation, forcing individuals and settlements into disconnected, taxable units. Restoring community ties directly counters this strategy. When villages learn to rely on mutual aid and collective strength, imperial decrees lose their authority, rendering bureaucratic occupation obsolete. The Vicar-Mediator uses their authority not to dominate or conquer, but to unburden communities, settle disputes, calibrate regional Spires and facilitate the organic flow of mutual aid.

### **2\. World Architecture, Exploration, Caravan Logistics, & Spire Calibration**

#### **2.1 Regional Architecture & Overworld Paper Map**

Rather than relying on an uncurated, sprawling open world, *The Ink and the Ash* utilizes large, highly structured regional zones connected by a tactile 3D Overworld Paper Map. This design choice guarantees high visual fidelity and deliberate pacing, drawing structural inspiration from classic JRPGs such as *Chrono Trigger* and *Final Fantasy XII*.

The Overworld Paper Map is presented as an antique traveler's ledger crafted from textured parchment. Unexplored territories are concealed beneath a dark ink-wash fog of war. As the caravan travels along physical roads and discovers hidden waystations, detailed ink-style illustrations dissolve onto the parchment page, transforming the map into a living record of the caravan's journey.

#### **2.2 Biome Matrix & Regional Spires**

The world comprises seven distinct biomes. Each biome houses an ancient regional Spire (tower) that anchors physical reality and calms environmental anomalies.

| Biome Name | Associated Spire | Environmental Hazards & Pressures | Logistical Requirements | Local Industries & Resources |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **The Grasslands** | Verdant Spire | Mild weather, rolling plains and gentle terrain. | Standard travel foot packs; starting hub region. | Pastoral agriculture, grain farming and livestock. |
| **The Coast** | Tidal Spire | Heavy sea fog, tidal flats and salt spray. | Moisture protection for cargo; fish preservation gear. | Maritime trading ports and fish preservation. |
| **The Forest** | Sylvan Spire | Dense old-growth canopy and dark winding trails. | Navigation tools and timber clearing equipment. | Timber camps, resin harvesting and hidden shrines. |
| **The Mountains** | Frost Spire | Freezing elevations and sudden cold snaps. | Specialized cold-weather gear and warming rations. | Glacier salt extraction and elemental ice alchemy. |
| **The Swamp** | Mire Spire | Thick mist, slow-moving waterways and treacherous bogstilts. | Antiseptic supplies and mud-traversing gear. | Alchemical flora harvesting and bog settlement trade. |
| **The Desert** | Sun Spire | Sun-baked dunes, extreme heat fatigue and sandstorms. | Shade management, hydration stores and heat linings. | Salt flats, clay harvesting and oasis trade posts. |
| **The Jungle** | Canopy Spire | Humid rain-canopies and overgrown stone ruins. | Botanical remedies and jungle cutting tools. | Rare botanical harvesting and ancient relic recovery. |

The pilgrimage is fully non-linear. Players may approach the seven regional Spires in any order. Progression gating is environmental and logistical rather than artificial; for example, attempting to ascend the Frost Spire without cold-weather clothing or warming meals results in rapid fatigue and systemic debuffs.

#### **2.3 Journey Weight, Time Mechanics, & Upgradable Caravan**

Fast travel is completely prohibited. The physical journey across the terrain forms the core gameplay loop, forcing players to carefully evaluate route planning, weather forecasts and inventory preservation before leaving safe havens.

* **Time Ratio:** Time advances at a ratio of 1 minute real-time to 1 hour in-game. A full 24-hour day completes in 24 minutes of real-time play, creating a structured daily rhythm of morning departures, mid-day travel, evening camping and overnight rest.  
* **Weather Multipliers & Numerical Decay Rates:** Dynamic weather introduces active operational pressure on perishable goods. Base real-time decay for raw organic items is set at 1% durability loss per real-time minute. Adverse environmental conditions apply severe acceleration multipliers:  
  * *Desert / Sun Spire Heat:* \+50% spoilage rate multiplier (1.5% durability loss/min).  
  * *Rain / Swamp Humidity:* \+50% spoilage and moisture weight multiplier (1.5% durability loss/min).  
  * *Freezing Temperatures:* Freezing reduces organic decay to 0.2% per minute but accelerates party stamina drain by 2.0x per travel unit.  
* **Caravan Progression & Capacity Limits:**  
  * *Foot Packs (Base Tier):* Capacity limit of 30 slots (50 kg maximum weight). Provides zero environmental protection, exposing all stored items to ambient decay multipliers.  
  * *Horse-Drawn Carriage (Upgraded Tier):* Capacity limit of 120 slots (300 kg maximum weight). Equipping dedicated storage boxes and alchemical cooling modules reduces ambient item decay rates by a flat 60% across all biomes.  
* **Horizontal Mastery Progression:** Character and account progression intentionally avoids vertical stat inflation (such as endless HP or raw attack power growth). Instead, advancement focuses entirely on horizontal expansion: acquiring hazard-resistant apparel, expanding CTB tactical options, fitting utility modules to the carriage and expanding regional community support networks.

#### **2.4 Spire Calibration Mechanics**

To restore stability to a biome, the Vicar-Mediator must perform a complete Spire Calibration sequence:

1. **Ascend the Peak:** Travel through the regional zone and climb to the highest chamber of the ancient Spire.  
2. **Clear Hazards:** Defeat or clear localized monsters and environmental corruption that have settled inside the tower structure.  
3. **Light the Beacon:** Activate the central light mechanism at the top of the Spire.

Calibrating a Spire alters the surrounding world state. The lit beacon actively repels monsters from the immediate sector, rendering nearby settlements safe and opening previously blocked regional trade routes between biomes.

### **3\. Combat System & Operational Recovery**

#### **3.1 Conditional Turn-Based (CTB) System & Timeline UI**

Combat in *The Ink and the Ash* uses a Conditional Turn-Based (CTB) system modeled after *Final Fantasy X*. Turn order is calculated continuously based on individual character speed attributes and the specific delay costs of executed skills, replacing static turn rounds.

A prominent timeline display positioned along the screen margin provides clear tactical foresight. Players can review exactly how queuing a technique will delay or accelerate upcoming turns for both party members and enemies. This transparency rewards careful planning, patience and defensive coordination over rapid button input.

The mathematical core governing turn order and skill execution delays relies on explicit formulas:

`Base Turn Delay = (Skill Cost / Agility Attribute) * Weather Multiplier`

`Next Turn Time = Current Time + Base Turn Delay`

\+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+  
\|                      CTB TIMELINE UI DISPLAY                          \|  
\|  Turn 01: Vicar-Mediator  \[Action: Anchor\] \--------\> (Links Allies)   \|  
\|  Turn 02: Companion A     \[Action: Skill Delay \+2\]                     \|  
\|  Turn 03: Companion B     \[Action: Sync Move\] \<---- (Guaranteed Sync) \|  
\|  Turn 04: Hostile Unit    \[Action: Attack\]                            \|  
\+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+

#### **3.2 Teamwork Mechanics: "Stronger Together" in Battle**

Combat abilities prioritize mutual aid and tactical collaboration, embodying the project's core narrative theme through explicit mechanics:

* **Sync Moves (Cooperative Abilities):** Triggered when two active party members align adjacently on the CTB timeline (defined as a `Turn Delta <= 2` timeline units). These joint actions combine complementary skill sets (such as fusing wind techniques with fire or applying protective shields during an attack) without expending extra turn slots.  
* **Interventions (Defensive Synergy):** When an enemy targets a vulnerable or low-health ally, a healthy companion can execute an Intervention. Execution requires spending 25 Focus Points to intercept the incoming strike, absorb damage or shift the targeted ally forward on the timeline by up to 15 timeline units.  
* **The Mediator's Anchor:** A specialized ability unique to the Vicar-Mediator. Expending a turn to cast Anchor links two chosen allies on the timeline, guaranteeing access to Sync Moves regardless of incoming speed debuffs or turn delays.

#### **3.3 Defeat, Injury Debuffs, & Recovery Loop**

Failing in combat does not result in a standard game-over screen. When the active party is defeated, the party wipes and reloads at the last established campsite.

Upon reloading, all active party members suffer temporary **Injury Debuffs** that reduce physical efficiency, slow timeline turn generation by 20% and increase skill delay costs by 15%. To clear these debuffs, players must engage in the campsite recovery loop:

1. Pitch camp in a safe overworld location.  
2. Rest for a full in-game sleep cycle (8 in-game hours).  
3. Prepare and consume restorative cooked meals using harvested ingredients.

### **4\. Party, Companion Dynamics, & Haven Progression**

#### **4.1 Active Party Limits & Composition Constraints**

While the Vicar-Mediator recruits a growing roster of companions across the seven biomes, active field exploration and combat enforce a **strict limit of 3 party members** (the protagonist plus two companions).

This operational limit prevents trivializing combat encounters and enforces intentional planning. Players must carefully select their two active companions based on the specific environmental hazards, speed profiles and Sync Move synergies needed for the upcoming region.

#### **4.2 Safe Haven Progression Restrictions**

Character management cannot be performed mid-journey while traversing wilderness zones. Strategic maintenance is restricted exclusively to safe havens:

* Spending accumulated insight points to unlock new tactical skill nodes.  
* Equipping or swapping weapons, defensive apparel and utility gear.  
* Swapping active companions in and out of the primary party limit.

These actions can **ONLY** be executed while resting at roadside campsites or town inns. In line with the design philosophy of horizontal mastery, spending insight points expands tactical CTB options, hazard resistance profiles, utility gear capabilities and campsite synergies rather than inflating raw HP or attack values.

#### **4.3 Roadside Burden-Sharing & Campfire Vignettes**

Travel weariness manifests psychologically during long journeys through contextual chatter and visual burden-sharing rather than penalizing player movement speeds. Companions swap heavy cargo packs during long treks, offer encouraging dialogue during harsh storms and lean on one another during exhausting climbs.

At camp, companion dynamics transition into the evening campsite loop:

* **Evening Chores:** Both active and bench companions handle essential duties, such as prepping food ingredients, repairing worn gear and tending carriage animals.  
* **Campfire Vignettes:** Gathering around the fire triggers character narrative scenes, deepening companion bonds and building relational trust.  
* **Morning Buffs:** Successful campsite care grants day-long stat buffs (with an explicit Buff Duration Window of 24 in-game hours) that improve combat turn speeds and environmental hazard resistance for the day ahead.

These companion relationships provide the personal emotional framework that connects directly to the broader narrative struggles of the villages encountered along the pilgrimage route.

### **5\. Narrative, Faction Architecture, & Technical NPC Simulation**

#### **5.1 The 5-Act NPC Quest Architecture**

Because fast travel is prohibited and Spire maintenance requires retracing travel routes, players regularly revisit settlements across the overworld paper map. To reward this back-and-forth travel, every key NPC follows a comprehensive **5-Act Quest Arc** that evolves over time.

\|         Initial local problem (resource scarcity, tax pressure)       \|  
\+-----------------------------------T-----------------------------------+  
                                    \|  
                                    v  
\+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+

\|          Unforeseen secondary community complications arise           \|  
\+-----------------------------------T-----------------------------------+  
                                    \|  
                                    v  
\+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+  
\|                        ACT III: THE CRISIS                            \|  
\|           Major emotional or physical mediator climax point           \|  
\+-----------------------------------T-----------------------------------+  
                                    \|  
                                    v  
\+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+  
\|                       ACT IV: THE AFTERMATH                           \|  
\|            Community processes fallout and adjusts structure          \|  
\+-----------------------------------T-----------------------------------+  
                                    \|  
                                    v  
\+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+  
\|                        ACT V: THE LEGACY                              \|

1. **Act I: The Friction:** The initial meeting. Introduces a grounded local problem tied to resource scarcity, neighborly friction or imperial overreach.  
2. **Act II: The Ripple:** Rising action. The player's initial intervention causes unforeseen secondary complications across the local community.  
3. **Act III: The Crisis:** The climax. A major physical or emotional turning point that requires formal mediator intervention and ethical decision-making.  
4. **Act IV: The Aftermath:** Falling action. The community processes systemic changes, adjusts to new realities and begins reconciliation.  
5. **Act V: The Legacy:** Resolution. The village undergoes a permanent physical transformation, unlocking unique crafting schematics, safe havens or regional boons.

#### **5.2 Internal Village Factions (The Micro-Matrix)**

Settlements are fractured by six internal factions. These groups create local tension until mediator intervention helps villagers recognize their mutual interdependence.

| Faction Name | Core Values & Priorities | Stance on Imperial Rule | Preferred Resolution Methods |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Traditionalists** | Historical customs, elder authority and heritage preservation. | Skeptical of change; resists external mandates. | Cultural preservation and ancestral rites. |
| **Pragmatists** | Practical survival, material stability and trade efficiency. | Tolerates empire if trade remains profitable. | Economic stability and resource management. |
| **Young Radicals** | Immediate systemic change and swift social disruption. | Active resistance and anti-imperial action. | Direct confrontation and political shifts. |
| **The Burdened** | Immediate relief, mental health care and shared physical labor. | Neutrally overwhelmed by physical taxation. | Mutual aid, unburdening and shared work. |
| **Outsiders** | Acceptance, integration and safe travel for itinerant merchants. | Seeks protection from imperial harassment. | Open trade routes and fair integration. |
| **Imperial Bureaucracy** | Legal compliance, tax collection and administrative control. | Occupying authority enforcing rigid stasis. | Audits, legal mandates and iron control. |

#### **5.3 Pixel Crushers Love/Hate Engine Integration**

Social simulation mechanics run on Pixel Crushers' Love/Hate engine, evaluating dynamic relationships across all settlements:

* **PAD Emotional Model:** Tracks three emotional axes for NPCs on a scale from \-100 to \+100: *Pleasure* (current mood), *Arousal* (level of agitation or panic) and *Dominance* (feeling of agency versus helplessness). These values shift dynamically based on campsite cooking choices, shared supplies and resolved disputes.  
* **Rumor Propagation:** Merchant caravans and roadside travelers carry NPC memories and judged deeds along trade roads as rumors. Distant villages alter their baseline trust toward the player based on these rumors before the caravan even arrives.  
* **Custom Relationship Vectors:** Tracks distinct relational metrics beyond simple affinity, specifically measuring local *Fear* toward the empire versus internal *Trust* between fractured village factions.

#### **6.3 Reciprocity & Anti-Imperial Resistance**

The economic loop culminates in the system of Reciprocity. *The Ink and the Ash* rejects monetary accumulation as a metric of success. Instead, helping settlements complete their 5-Act Quest Arcs transforms regional economics and provides concrete mechanical payoffs:

* **Free Maintenance Services:** Completing Act V of a village arc unlocks free carriage structural repairs and wheel reinforcements at local smithies, eliminating scrap iron maintenance costs.  
* **Alchemical Craftables:** Fully resolved village networks grant specialized craftables, such as alchemical cooling linings synthesized from Glacier Salt and Solar Clay that permanently cut carriage spoilage rates.  
* **Imperial Patrol Distractions:** Strong community solidarity triggers local distraction events that permanently clear Imperial Bureaucracy patrols along specific trade roads, securing safe passage for trade shipments.

By building up communities through mutual aid, the Vicar-Mediator demonstrates that cooperative care directly overcomes cold imperial bureaucracy, establishing a living world where resistance and restoration are one and the same.

### **7\. Technical Architecture, Audio Strategy, & System Refinements**

#### **7.1 Unity 6 URP & Android/PC Cross-Platform Performance Architecture**

The game targets Unity 6 utilizing the Universal Render Pipeline (URP), engineered specifically to achieve a consistent 60 FPS performance target across both Android mobile devices and Windows PC builds. Technical optimization strategies include:

* **Asset Instancing & Occlusion:** Implementation of GPU instancing across Synty Fantasy Village and Biome asset packages, paired with aggressive occlusion culling to minimize render batch counts.  
* **Platform-Specific Compression:** Utilization of ASTC texture compression for Android hardware and BC7 compression for PC builds to optimize VRAM footprint.  
* **Lighting & Memory Management:** High-fidelity baked lightmaps augmented by a single lightweight real-time directional light, alongside managed garbage collection systems to eliminate runtime frame stutters during overworld travel.

#### **7.2 Google Lyria Dynamic Audio & Sound Design Strategy**

Audio architecture leverages a Google Lyria-generated adaptive soundtrack system. Each of the seven regional biomes includes dedicated exploration themes that seamlessly morph into active, rhythmic arrangements upon entering combat transitions. Custom tracks cover settled villages, major cities, and Spire ascents. Complementary sound effects (SFX) for combat impacts, interface clicks, ambient weather, and caravan motion are sourced from a curated Unity audio asset pack.

#### **7.3 Streamlined List-Based Inventory & Campsite Cooking**

Caravan storage uses a streamlined, list-based inventory interface, shifting the focus of logistical gameplay toward environmental route management and resource preservation rather than grid-fitting puzzle mechanics. Campsite cooking is simplified to direct recipe selection from available unified ingredients, immediately granting day-long expedition buffs upon meal preparation.

#### **7.4 Platform-Agnostic Input & UI Architecture**

A unified input abstraction layer provides seamless cross-platform control, supporting touch screen gestures (virtual joysticks and tap-to-move options) for Android devices alongside mouse/keyboard and gamepad configurations for PC. The resolution-independent URP UI canvas layout scales dynamically to maintain optimal legibility and ergonomic button layouts across phone displays and desktop monitors.

* **Accessibility-First Input Design & Motor Accessibility:** Primary overworld navigation utilizes point-and-click mouse controls (left-click to move/interact) on PC and touch tap-to-move on mobile, minimizing complex physical dexterity requirements. Input controls support complete remapping across keyboard, mouse, and gamepads, ensuring seamless compatibility with adaptive controller setups.  
* **Combat Toggles & Interaction Magnetism:** The CTB combat system maintains active tactical urgency while strictly avoiding complex key modifiers and utilizing toggle states for persistent actions instead of requiring continuous button holding. Raycast hitboxes for environmental objects, NPCs, and gathering nodes are significantly widened with smart interaction magnetism, removing the need for exact pixel precision during clicks or taps.  
* **Automated Overworld Navigation:** An optional "Auto-Follow Road" navigation feature allows players to set waypoints on the 3D Overworld Paper Map, enabling automated caravan travel along established roads without continuous physical steering input.


### **8\. Implementation Architecture \& Resolved Design Decisions**

The following decisions define the concrete technical and production architecture used to realize the design vision described in sections 1 through 7. Each decision was evaluated against the project's core requirements: 60 FPS on Android and PC, cozy-melancholic aesthetic fidelity, horizontal mastery progression and restorative stewardship gameplay.

#### **8.1 Project Foundation \& Build Pipeline**

| Decision Domain | Resolved Choice | Rationale |

#### **8.2 Runtime Architecture \& System Communication**

| Decision Domain | Resolved Choice | Rationale |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Event Architecture** | ScriptableObject-based event channels. One ScriptableObject asset per event type. Listeners subscribe in `OnEnable` and unsubscribe in `OnDisable`. | Zero-allocation, inspector-debuggable and fully decoupled. Systems communicate without direct references, supporting clean scene transitions and modular testing. |
| **JSON Serialization** | Dual strategy: Unity's built-in `JsonUtility` for simple flat data structures; Newtonsoft Json.NET (`com.unity.nuget.newtonsoft-json`) for nested and complex structures including dialogue trees, quest arcs and PAD emotional state graphs. | `JsonUtility` provides zero-allocation fast parsing for runtime-hot paths (item lookups, stat reads). Json.NET handles polymorphic dialogue branches, dictionary-based faction maps and nullable fields that `JsonUtility` cannot process. |
| **State Persistence** | Persistent `GameManager` singleton (`DontDestroyOnLoad`) holding references to ScriptableObject-based runtime data stores. Systems read their state from the shared data store on scene load. | Guarantees inventory, quest progress, time-of-day, PAD emotional states and caravan upgrade data survive full scene swaps between the overworld map and biome scenes. |
| **Disk Save System** | Pixel Crushers Save System (bundled with Dialogue System for Unity). | Natively hooks into Dialogue System quest states and Love/Hate faction data. Supports JSON disk serialization with named save slot management. Eliminates the need to build a custom save pipeline. |
| **Testing Framework** | Unity Test Framework (NUnit-based) for Edit Mode unit tests covering CTB formula calculations, decay math, quest state transitions and JSON deserialization. Play Mode tests for integration testing of scene transitions and combat flows. | Automated regression coverage for all core numerical systems. Play Mode tests validate full gameplay loops without manual play-testing overhead. |

#### **8.4 Gameplay Systems Architecture**

| Decision Domain | Resolved Choice | Rationale |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Navigation \& Pathfinding** | Unity NavMesh system. Baked NavMesh surfaces per scene. Click or tap sets a destination; `NavMeshAgent` handles pathfinding and obstacle avoidance. WASD and gamepad stick input function as parallel directional overrides. | Reliable pathfinding with minimal configuration. NavMesh baking integrates cleanly with Synty environment props. Point-and-click destination setting and WASD directional control coexist without input conflicts. |
| **Combat Staging** | Seamless in-world combat. No scene transition upon encounter. The camera reframes, CTB timeline UI overlays appear and turn-based flow activates at the physical encounter location. The world remains loaded throughout. | Preserves environmental immersion and travel continuity. The player sees the terrain, weather and time-of-day context during combat, reinforcing the connection between journey logistics and tactical decisions. Eliminates loading interruptions for frequent wildlife encounters. |
| **Encounter Triggers** | Visible enemies patrolling, grazing or guarding areas in the world. Contact or proximity triggers the combat transition. | Grants the player agency to choose engagement timing, plan approaches and avoid fights when resources are low. Visible enemies reinforce the living-world simulation and support the design philosophy of deliberate, non-random pilgrimage pacing. |
| **Party Visibility** | Three active party members are visible during exploration. The protagonist leads with two companions trailing in formation. During combat, companions spread to tactical positions around the engagement point. | Visual companion presence reinforces the "Stronger Together" narrative theme during travel. The trailing formation provides clear spatial feedback on party composition and enables roadside burden-sharing animations and contextual chatter. |
| **Dialogue \& Quest System** | Pixel Crushers Dialogue System for Unity, natively integrated with Pixel Crushers Love/Hate. Supports JSON-driven dialogue trees, quest state machines and a visual node editor for content authoring. | Single integrated solution covering dialogue execution, quest tracking (5-Act Architecture), PAD emotional state evaluation and rumor propagation. The visual node editor accelerates narrative content creation for 200+ NPCs across seven biomes. |
| **UI Framework** | Unity UI (Canvas/UGUI). | Mature ecosystem with extensive community resources and third-party asset compatibility. Pixel Crushers Dialogue System ships with built-in UGUI prefabs for dialogue panels, quest logs and response menus. Canvas-based layout provides reliable cross-platform scaling for both mobile phone displays and desktop monitors. |

#### **8.5 Scene \& World Management**

| Decision Domain | Resolved Choice | Rationale |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Scene Structure** | One dedicated scene per biome (7 biome scenes total). Full scene swap with a loading screen when crossing biome borders. | Simplifies memory management by guaranteeing only one biome's worth of assets occupies VRAM at any time. Clean scene boundaries align with the distinct environmental identities of each biome. Loading screens provide natural pacing transitions between geographic regions. |
| **Overworld Paper Map** | A separate dedicated 3D scene. The parchment map renders as a physical 3D environment with ink-wash fog of war shaders, a node-based road network and a miniature caravan token. Selecting a destination and arriving at a town or zone border triggers a scene load into the corresponding biome scene. | Isolates the stylized parchment rendering from biome-realistic rendering. The dedicated scene allows custom shaders, fog dissolve effects and hand-drawn illustration reveals without competing for rendering resources with active biome environments. |
